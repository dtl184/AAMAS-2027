\paragraph{Description-length prior.} With demonstrations from a single store, the coordinate rule and the intended
norm are almost indistinguishable by likelihood: the log-likelihood difference is $0.005$ nats in every run. Selection
is therefore decided by the description-length prior. Our default coding charges a coordinate literal
\texttt{at(x,y)} one unit but a numeric comparison three, which makes memorisation cheap. Re-scoring the identical
GPT-5.5 candidate sets with a token-consistent coding (coordinates cost three units; likelihood unchanged) raised the
posterior mass on intended-equivalent norms in the single-layout condition (by $+0.12$ and $+0.10$ for the two guidance
variants, paired 95\% CIs excluding zero). It also removed the spurious coordinate MAP in 6/10 and 3/10 runs. However,
the intended norm became the MAP in only 1/20 runs. The definitions GPT-5.5 proposed were usually long (17--24 units),
so they still lost to the 16-unit coordinate rule. Most freed runs selected other relational rules, some of which
over-generalised to the test layout. With two training layouts, results were unchanged (intended norm selected in
5/10 runs). Consistent complexity accounting removes an artificial bias toward coordinates, but on its own it does not
make single-layout norm discovery reliable.
